LENS 01Operations & securities servicing

The corporate-actions view

How a custodian, transfer agent, or back-office team would code, communicate, and process this event — including the bilateral break-up structure and the escrowed debt.

Event classification

A stock-for-stock merger of equals with a target-side stockholder election. The election is voluntary; the exchange itself is mandatory upon close.1,2

  • Surviving listed issuer: Getty Images Holdings, Inc. (NYSE: GETY).
  • Shutterstock (NYSE: SSTK) shares are cancelled at close; the line is delisted and the CUSIP retired.
  • Cash election fixed at $28.84870/share; stock election fixed at 13.67237 Getty shares; mixed election fixed at $9.50 cash plus 9.17 Getty shares.

Mechanics ops teams must monitor

  • Election window and default: holders who make no valid election are generally treated as making a mixed election.
  • Proration: cash elections are prorated if the cash pool is oversubscribed; stock elections are prorated if the stock pool is oversubscribed.
  • Fractional shares: no fractional Getty shares will be issued; fractional entitlements are settled in cash in lieu.
  • Tax treatment: the parties intend the transaction to qualify as a tax-deferred reorganization for U.S. federal income tax purposes. Cash received, including cash in lieu of fractional shares, may cause taxable gain recognition. Not tax advice.

Break-up fee mechanics

The bilateral termination structure matters for risk management on both sides.16

  • If Shutterstock pursues a superior proposal: Shutterstock pays Getty $32.7M.
  • If Getty terminates and completes a competing deal within 12 months: Getty pays Shutterstock $32.7M.
  • If Getty cannot fund the cash consideration (financing termination right): Getty pays Shutterstock $40.0M.
  • Both fees cannot be triggered simultaneously; Getty's maximum exposure is $40M.

A second event on the debt side

The deal spawned its own debt corporate action: $628.4M of 10.500% senior secured notes issued and escrowed.6

  • If the merger is not consummated by 6 Oct 2026, the notes are subject to special mandatory redemption at 100% of issue price plus accrued and unpaid interest.
  • Interest is scheduled semiannually on 15 May and 15 November, beginning 15 May 2026; 6 Oct 2026 is the merger outside-date redemption trigger, not a scheduled interest-payment date.
  • A live example of acquisition financing riding alongside an equity event, with its own redemption trigger for fixed-income servicing teams.

The CMA divestiture as a corporate action

The clearance condition requires Shutterstock to divest its entire global editorial business to a CMA-approved buyer before the merger can close.9,10

  • An offer to sell only Backgrid and Splash (celebrity/paparazzi) was rejected — the CMA found too much overlap with the broader standard editorial operation.
  • No CMA-approved purchaser has been publicly named as of 1 June 2026.
  • The October 2026 outside date creates material time pressure on the sale process.
LENS 02Strategy & value creation

The M&A advisory view

How a strategy adviser would frame the rationale, stress-test the synergies, and flag the structural risks — including what the CMA remedy does to the value thesis.

Strategic rationale

Scale-driven consolidation in a maturing market facing a generative-AI demand shock. Combining complementary creative, editorial, and footage libraries widens the catalogue, deepens exclusive event coverage, and creates cross-sell into subscription revenue, which represents roughly 46% of pro-forma 2024 revenue.1

Value-creation thesis

  • Cost synergies: $150–200M annual across SG&A and capex by year three.
  • Accretion: earnings and cash-flow accretive from year two.
  • Financial scale: pro-forma 2024 revenue of $1.98–1.99B; pre-synergy EBITDA $569–574M.1
  • Leverage: ~3.0× net leverage at signing, before the high-yield financing stack raised the debt load further.

Synergy and integration analysis

Most near-term synergy value sits in overlapping SG&A and technology platforms rather than revenue uplift. A diligence team would stress-test for revenue dis-synergies: customer overlap, contributor churn, and the morale of two large creator ecosystems through a prolonged, contested integration.

The remedy risk baked into the deal

The CMA's requirement to divest Shutterstock's entire global editorial business is a textbook illustration of remedy risk eroding the original deal thesis. The editorial business was part of the scale argument — selling it to clear the deal reduces the very combination that justified the premium and the synergy estimates.

Risks a diligence team would flag

  • AI substitution: generative AI is reducing licensing demand from creative buyers — the structural question over the entire thesis.
  • Expensive financing: coupon range of 10.5%–14% across the capital structure.12,13
  • Divestiture execution: finding a CMA-approved buyer for a whole editorial business within the Oct 2026 outside date is a live, material risk.
  • Break-up optionality: the $40M financing termination fee sets a floor on Getty's cost of walking away if debt conditions deteriorate.

Why this structure signals negotiating parity

The 54.7/45.3 ownership split, the 11-member board with four Shutterstock seats, and the bilateral break-up fees are all markers of a genuine merger of equals. In a standard acquisition the target fee stands alone; here both sides carry exposure — a structural acknowledgment that each party's consent matters all the way to close.

LENS 03Multi-jurisdiction antitrust

Regulators in international M&A

The defining feature of this deal: the US DOJ cleared it unconditionally; the UK CMA required a structural divestiture. Same transaction, opposite conclusions.

United States

DOJ — clean clearance

The Department of Justice let the HSR waiting period expire without conditions on 23 February 2026. No remedies, no divestiture, no behavioural commitments.3

OUTCOME → Unconditional clearance.
United Kingdom

CMA — structural remedy

After Phase 1 referral and an in-depth Phase 2 investigation under the Enterprise Act 2002, the CMA conditionally cleared on 15 May 2026. Clearance requires divesting Shutterstock's entire global editorial business to a CMA-approved buyer.9,10

OUTCOME → Conditional clearance. Full editorial divestiture required.

Why the outcomes diverged

Both regulators agreed the global stock-content market raised no concerns. The split turns on the UK editorial imagery market. The CMA judged Shutterstock is one of the few meaningful alternatives to Getty for UK media outlets, and that the combination would reduce competition there materially.3,9

Structural vs behavioural remedies

The CMA rejected five-year behavioural undertakings (price caps, product-range commitments) and the narrower Backgrid/Splash-only offer. Only a full structural divestiture of the broader editorial business would restore the competition Shutterstock currently provides.8,9,10

Process lessons for dealmakers

  • Remedies tabled on the final permitted day left the CMA insufficient time to assess complex commitments — a procedural misstep that hardened the referral.7,8
  • Behavioural remedies face monitoring, circumvention, and specification risks that the CMA consistently disfavours for enduring concerns.
  • Early, substantive remedy engagement is now table stakes in UK Phase 1.

The bigger picture

Despite government pressure for a more pro-investment posture on deals also cleared in the US, the CMA acted robustly. This deal is a teaching case: a global transaction is only as approved as its strictest jurisdiction.7,8,9
