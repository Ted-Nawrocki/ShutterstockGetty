LENS 01Operations & securities servicing

The corporate actions view

How a custodian, transfer agent, or back-office team would code, communicate, and process this event -- and what the termination means for every team that spent 18 months preparing to service it.

The election event: 18 months open, closed with nothing to process

The merger termination unwinds the entire stockholder election event. No consideration will be paid. No SSTK shares will be exchanged.18

  • Cash elections at $28.84870 per share: unwound.
  • Stock elections at 13.67237 Getty shares per share: unwound.
  • Mixed elections at $9.50 cash plus 9.17 Getty shares: unwound.
  • Every proration calculation, cash in lieu model, and tax lot analysis built for this event is now moot.
  • SSTK remains listed on NYSE as a standalone company. The CUSIP is not retired.

The debt event: special mandatory redemption triggered

The termination triggers a separate, distinct corporate action on the debt side.6,18

  • Getty's $628.4M 10.500% senior secured notes due 2030 are subject to special mandatory redemption under the indenture dated 21 October 2025.
  • Redemption price: 100% of principal plus accrued and unpaid interest.
  • Interest is scheduled semiannually on 15 May and 15 November. The carry cost of holding these notes in escrow for roughly nine months is a real, sunk cost to Getty.
  • Fixed-income servicing teams must process the redemption and update position records accordingly.

Event classification

As originally structured: a stock-for-stock merger of equals with a target-side stockholder election. The election was voluntary; the exchange would have been mandatory upon close.1,2

  • Surviving listed issuer would have been: Getty Images Holdings, Inc. (NYSE: GETY).
  • Shutterstock (NYSE: SSTK) shares would have been cancelled at close; the line delisted and the CUSIP retired.
  • Neither has happened. Both lines remain active.

Break-up fee: what the primary filings say

The merger agreement contains bilateral termination fees. Whether any fee is payable in this specific termination scenario is a nuanced legal question.16,18

  • The Form 8-K and Form 425 filed 30 June 2026 describe termination as occurring at the Second Extended End Date -- consistent with an outside date lapse, which does not trigger a termination fee under the merger agreement.
  • Some secondary sources assert Getty owes Shutterstock $40M. This claim has not been confirmed in either company's primary SEC filings reviewed for this site.
  • Getty's maximum contractual fee exposure was $40M (financing termination right) or $32.7M (rival deal scenario). Both sides' advisors and lawyers are paid regardless.

LENS 02Strategy & value creation

The M&A advisory view

How a strategy adviser would frame the rationale, what the deal was supposed to deliver, and what the termination leaves behind.

Strategic rationale

Scale-driven consolidation in a maturing market facing a generative-AI demand shock. Combining complementary creative, editorial, and footage libraries was intended to widen the catalogue, deepen exclusive event coverage, and create cross-sell into subscription revenue, which represented roughly 46% of pro-forma 2024 revenue.1

Value-creation thesis -- never realized

  • Cost synergies: $150--200M annual across SG&A and capex by year three. Not realized.
  • Accretion: Earnings and cash-flow accretive from year two. Not realized.
  • Financial scale: Pro-forma 2024 revenue of $1.98--1.99B; pre-synergy EBITDA $569--574M. Never combined.1

The remedy risk that killed the deal

The CMA's condition was not a negotiating position. It was structural: divest the entire global editorial business -- Rex Features, Splash News, and Backgrid -- to a buyer who did not yet exist and would need CMA approval. That condition eliminated the very scale advantage the merger was designed to create. Getty's board correctly identified that accepting the condition made the deal not worth doing.

What the termination leaves behind

  • Getty carrying approximately $2 billion in total debt, now exploring "strategic financing alternatives."
  • $628.4M in notes to be redeemed at par plus accrued interest -- a sunk carry cost.
  • 18 months of management bandwidth, advisory fees, and legal costs on both sides: unrecoverable.
  • Both companies facing the same AI disruption threat they tried to address through scale, now as smaller standalone businesses.

The AI irony

The deal was partly motivated by the competitive threat of AI-generated imagery. Within weeks of termination, Getty had signed a display licensing deal with OpenAI (images in ChatGPT) and Shutterstock reaffirmed its training data deal with OpenAI (powering DALL-E).19 Two companies that tried to merge to fight AI are now individually negotiating with the same AI platform -- on opposite sides of the content relationship.

Why the structure signalled negotiating parity

The 54.7/45.3 ownership split, the 11-member board with four Shutterstock seats, and the bilateral break-up fees were all markers of a genuine merger of equals. In a standard acquisition the target fee stands alone; here both sides carried exposure -- a structural acknowledgment that each party's consent mattered. Ultimately it was regulatory consent that was never fully obtained.

LENS 03Multi-jurisdiction antitrust

Regulators in international M&A

The DOJ cleared it. The CMA killed it. The definitive case study in multi-jurisdiction regulatory risk.

United States

DOJ -- clean clearance

The Department of Justice let the HSR waiting period expire without conditions on 23 February 2026. No remedies, no divestiture, no behavioural commitments.3

OUTCOME -- Unconditional clearance. Deal could have closed on US terms alone.
United Kingdom

CMA -- impossible condition

After Phase 1 referral and an in-depth Phase 2 investigation under the Enterprise Act 2002, the CMA conditionally cleared on 15 May 2026. Clearance required divesting Shutterstock's entire global editorial business -- Rex Features, Splash News, and Backgrid -- to a CMA-approved buyer. Getty's board rejected this condition on 30 June 2026.9,10,18

OUTCOME -- Conditional clearance rejected. Merger terminated 06 Jul 2026.

Why the outcomes diverged

Both regulators agreed the global stock-content market raised no concerns. The split turns on the UK editorial imagery market. The CMA judged Shutterstock is one of the few meaningful alternatives to Getty for UK media outlets -- specifically through Rex Features, Splash News, and Backgrid -- and that the combination would remove that competition.3,9

Why the condition was unworkable

The CMA rejected five-year behavioural undertakings and the parties' Backgrid and Splash-only offer. The required full editorial divestiture meant finding an unidentified, unapproved buyer for a significant business unit within a four-month window. No buyer was ever named. The condition was structurally impossible to satisfy on the timeline available.8,9,10

Process lessons for dealmakers

  • Remedies tabled on the final permitted day left the CMA insufficient time to assess complex commitments -- a procedural misstep that hardened the referral.7,8
  • Behavioural remedies face monitoring, circumvention, and specification risks that the CMA consistently disfavours for enduring concerns.
  • Early, substantive remedy engagement is table stakes in UK Phase 1. Late submission is not a strategy.

The lesson this deal will be taught with

The DOJ cleared it unconditionally. The CMA imposed a condition no rational board would accept. The deal died. This is the definitive modern example of the principle that a global transaction is only as approved as its strictest jurisdiction -- and that regulatory risk is a timeline risk, a financing risk, and an operational risk, not just a legal one.

Strategic financing alternatives -- what that phrase means

Getty's board has stated it will retain a financial advisor to evaluate strategic financing alternatives. In context, this is not routine language. Getty enters the post-merger period carrying approximately $2 billion in total debt, without the $150--200M in annual synergies this deal was supposed to generate, and with a capital structure that was built around a transaction that no longer exists. Credit analysts and capital markets professionals will read this language carefully. Getty's Q1 2026 results disclosed total debt of approximately $2.0 billion as of 31 March 2026.
