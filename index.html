/ 01 -- The transaction

Deal overview

Getty Images and Shutterstock agreed to combine in a stock-for-stock merger of equals in January 2025. The deal never closed. It was terminated July 6, 2026 after the UK CMA imposed a structural remedy condition that Getty's board rejected unanimously.1,2,18

What was proposed -- and what happened to it

Getty Images would have been the surviving listed issuer (NYSE: GETY), with Getty stockholders holding approximately 54.7% and Shutterstock stockholders approximately 45.3% of the fully diluted combined company. Shutterstock shareholders had the choice of cash, stock, or mixed consideration -- all subject to proration. That election event ran open for eighteen months. It was unwound on July 6, 2026 with nothing to process: no consideration paid, no shares exchanged, no CUSIP retired. SSTK remains listed as an independent company. The merger agreement contained bilateral termination fees, but termination at the outside date on regulatory grounds does not trigger those provisions per the primary Form 8-K. No fee is payable between the parties.1,2,16,18

The outcome
Two companies tried to merge to fight generative AI. A regulator with a pen stopped them. Now one of them is talking to lenders about whether it survives at all.
A bureaucrat's impossible condition. A company in distress. The operations teams are not done yet.

LENS 01Operations & securities servicing

The corporate actions view

This deal generated three distinct corporate actions events: a merger election that ran open for 18 months and closed with nothing to process, a debt redemption event, and now a potential bankruptcy reorganisation that would start a new workflow entirely.

The election unwind

The merger termination closed the Shutterstock stockholder election event with no processing required. No consideration was paid, no SSTK shares were exchanged, and no CUSIP was retired. SSTK remains listed on NYSE as an independent company.

Eighteen months of open exposure -- across all election types, proration mechanics, and cash in lieu calculations built for this event -- went away on July 6, 2026.

The mandatory redemption

The termination triggered a standalone debt corporate action. Getty's $628.4M 10.500% senior secured notes due 2030 were subject to special mandatory redemption under the indenture dated 21 October 2025.6,18

  • Redemption price: 100% of principal plus accrued and unpaid interest.
  • The carry cost of holding these notes in escrow for nine months is a sunk cost.
  • Fixed-income servicing teams must process the redemption and update position records.

What DIP financing means for ops teams

If Getty files, a Chapter 11 proceeding would generate a new set of events -- more complex than the original merger.20

  • DIP financing ranks senior to all existing debt: a new security with its own CUSIP, rate, and servicing requirements.
  • Automatic stay upon filing stops existing creditor enforcement actions across the capital structure.
  • Plan of reorganisation if confirmed produces its own distribution mechanics.
  • Debt-to-equity conversion would restructure the entire cap table -- its own event for equity servicing teams.
LENS 02Multi-jurisdiction antitrust

Regulators in international M&A

The DOJ cleared it. The CMA imposed a condition no board would accept. The deal died and the company entered distress. The definitive case study in multi-jurisdiction regulatory risk.

United States

DOJ -- clean clearance

HSR waiting period expired without conditions on 23 February 2026. No remedies, no divestiture, no behavioural commitments. The deal could have closed on US terms alone.3

OUTCOME -- Unconditional clearance.
United Kingdom

CMA -- impossible condition

Conditionally cleared 15 May 2026, requiring divestiture of Shutterstock's entire global editorial business -- Rex Features, Splash News, and Backgrid -- to a CMA-approved buyer. No buyer existed. Getty's board rejected the condition on 30 June 2026.9,10,18

OUTCOME -- Condition rejected. Deal terminated 06 Jul 2026. Getty in DIP talks 28 Sep 2026.

Why the condition was unworkable

The CMA rejected five-year behavioural undertakings and the Backgrid and Splash-only offer. Full editorial divestiture required finding an unidentified, unapproved buyer for a significant business unit in under four months. It was structurally impossible on the available timeline.8,9,10

The strategic rationale for the merger centred on editorial scale. Requiring divestiture of the editorial business eliminated the core value of the transaction. Getty's board correctly identified that accepting the condition made the deal not worth doing.

The consequences no regulator modelled

The CMA's stated purpose was protecting competition in the UK editorial photography market. What followed: a terminated merger, $628.4M in notes redeemed, a revolver fully drawn, credit ratings at CCC and Caa3, lenders organised with their own counsel and financial advisers within two weeks, and DIP financing talks by September.

The regulator that imposed the condition has no equity at stake and faces no accountability for the financial consequences. This deal will be taught as the definitive example that a global transaction is only as approved as its strictest jurisdiction -- and that regulatory risk is a credit risk and an existence risk, not just a legal one.

What "strategic financing alternatives" turned into

When Getty terminated the merger in July, the company announced it would retain a financial advisor to evaluate strategic financing alternatives. That phrase is not boilerplate. Getty entered the post-merger period carrying approximately $2 billion in total debt, without the $150--200M in annual synergies the deal was supposed to generate, and without the $162M cash benefit expected from the Shutterstock combination. By 8 July, lenders had retained Gibson Dunn. By 15 July, Houlihan Lokey was on board. By September, S&P and Moody's had cut the ratings to distressed territory. By 28 September, Bloomberg was reporting confidential DIP financing talks. We have not located a formal going concern qualification in Getty's primary filings and make no such claim. The Bloomberg reporting cited people familiar with the matter.11,20,21
